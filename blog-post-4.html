Is the automatic car wash bad for my vehicle?

A single pass and your paint is already paying the price. Here's exactly what each step of the “convenient” tunnel wash is doing to your investment.

G Griffin O'Connor Founder · March 15, 2026 · 8 min read

Above · A daily driver after a proper hand wash — the kind of finish a tunnel will quietly destroy.

It seems like automatic car washes are popping up everywhere in Peoria these days. We live in a world of ever-growing automation and convenience. But when it comes to your vehicle — which for most people is the second-biggest investment after the house — we should look closer at what that “convenience” is actually costing you. The honest answer doesn't look good.

What if I told you that after a single pass through an automatic car wash, your vehicle is already showing damage? It's true. The fact that the vehicle looks cleaner on the surface is how they reel you in. Here's what's actually going on underneath.

Recycled water

Tunnels recycle their water. That means fine particles of dirt, sand, and other contaminants get blasted onto your vehicle through high-pressure water jets — damaging the painted surfaces on a microscopic level with every pass. The car you brought in didn't have those particles. The car going out does.

Cheap cleaning detergents

The detergents used in tunnels do not properly decontaminate your vehicle and, over time, diminish your paint's clarity. They strip whatever wax or sealant you had on the paint, leaving the clear coat unprotected and exposed.

Wash cloths

The hanging cloth strips and foam “mitts” in a tunnel continuously pick up contaminants from previous vehicles and transfer them to yours. That's how scratches and swirl marks appear on a paint job that was perfect the week before. You're effectively running sandpaper across your paint.

Bristle brushes

Extremely aggressive. Commonly used on the sides and lower rocker panels because those areas pick up more dirt from being in the firing line of the wheels. Swirl marks are visible after a single wash. Use it weekly for a month and you've created the kind of damage that requires a multi-stage paint correction to fix.

“Cheap” protection products

The tier-upgrade waxes and “ceramic” finishes tunnels push at the kiosk do not meaningfully protect your vehicle from UV, acidic bug guts, brake dust, road salt, or the other contaminants that quietly destroy paint. They make the car look slightly shinier for 24 hours. That's it.

Here's the math nobody runs.

You buy a $60,000 vehicle. You get a tunnel-wash membership and run it through weekly. You won't notice for a couple of months. But you will start to see contaminants that don't rinse off, wheels that never come fully clean, and paint that becomes visibly swirled and oxidized. The longer it goes on, the worse it gets — and the more your trade-in value silently drops.

Where we land on this

It's wild to us how often people spend tens of thousands of dollars on a vehicle, drive it unprotected, wash it in a way that actively damages it, and then trade it in for pennies a few years later.

That's why our focus at O'Connor Detailing is on educating our community about proper vehicle care. If you want your vehicle to maintain a like-new condition for as long as you own it, we can help you build a hand-wash routine, recommend the right tools, and walk you through the small habits that protect what you've invested in.

Skip the tunnel. Pick up the phone — we'd rather talk you through how to do this right than have you damage your paint guessing.
